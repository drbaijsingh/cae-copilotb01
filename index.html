The daily reality

Three hours of your day, every day

Nobody struggles with the physics. They struggle with the eight hundred lines of syntax between them and the physics.

"What's the keyword for this again?"

You know exactly what analysis you need. You spend forty minutes in the documentation finding out how to ask for it, then another twenty because you got one flag wrong.

"Too many attempts made for this increment"

Eleven words that tell you nothing. The real cause is a node you can't see, in a file you haven't opened, three thousand lines down.

"It converged, so it's fine."

The most expensive sentence in engineering. A converged answer is not a correct one, and nothing in your solver will ever tell you the difference.

Templates and closed-form solutions

Every script comes from a fixed template. Every frequency comes from a published closed-form solution — Navier for simply supported plates, Warburton (1954) otherwise.

Where the method doesn't apply, it says so and stops. The Error Doctor returns nothing rather than guessing at a cause it doesn't recognise.

Before you ask

Reasonable questions

Is the tool really free?

Yes, and there is no sign-up. It runs entirely in your browser — nothing you type is sent anywhere. The paid things are the cohort and the consulting, because those are the parts that need my hours.

Can I trust a script I didn't write?

Not blindly, and the tool says so on every screen. Every script is commented so you can read what it does before you run it, and the analytical cross-check exists precisely so you have an independent number to compare against. Treat it as a fast, careful first draft from someone who has written this deck many times — not as an answer.

What can't it do yet?

Flat rectangular plates only. No composites, laminates or functionally graded materials yet — those are next, and they are the area I publish in. LS-DYNA gets you the control, material and boundary cards but you bring your own mesh. Downloads arrive as .txt, so rename them.

I'm a student. Is the cohort worth it over free tutorials?

If your goal is to see which buttons to click, YouTube is free and adequate. If your goal is to be hired as an analyst, the gap is judgement — knowing that a converged run can still be wrong, why your mesh isn't converged, and how to defend a number to someone senior. That is what six weekends with feedback buys, and it is not on YouTube.

Do you take small jobs?

Yes. A single component check is a normal piece of work and is priced accordingly. If a job is too small to be worth quoting, I will tell you and usually point you at the way to do it yourself.

Try the tool first

It's free and takes about a minute. If it saves you an afternoon, the cohort will save you a year.
